MOSAIC

Non-sequential, skill-centric planning for long-horizon manipulation

MOSAIC is a line of work on non-sequential planning for long-horizon manipulation with a library of skills. Instead of extending a plan step by step from the start or the goal, skills are used to find where they succeed on their own, those pieces are connected into a full plan, and every step is checked against a world model. This page collects the papers in this line.

Approach

Long-horizon tasks often need intermediate steps that sequential planners, which extend a plan one step at a time, struggle to find, such as pushing a plate to the table's edge before it can be grasped. MOSAIC searches non-sequentially: skill trajectories are found wherever they succeed and connected later, so the skills themselves guide the search.

Generators

Skills such as push, pull and pick are invoked wherever they are likely to succeed, producing trajectories (“islands of competence”) that need not be reachable from the start yet.

Connectors

Skills that solve boundary value problems between two world states, linking independently generated trajectories into a plan from start to goal.

World model

Skill outcomes are evaluated with a world model (a physics simulator, a learned model, or both) instead of hand-specified preconditions and effects.

MOSAIC

Skill-Centric Manipulation Planning with Physics Simulation

Introduces generators and connectors, with a physics simulator as the world model. Evaluated in simulation and on a real robot with diffusion-model, motion-planning and grasping skills.

Lazy-MOSAIC

Accelerating Skill-Centric Manipulation Planning with Learned Skill-Effect World Models

Searches over skill outcomes predicted by a learned skill-effect model and uses the simulator only to validate promising skills, reducing simulator queries and planning time.
